# Unlockstones — the weekly currency that gates every trait

**Unlocking a trait costs Unlockstones, and Unlockstones are obtainable only weekly.** That makes
them the real pacing constraint on the whole trait system — not Sollant, and not the drop.

| Unlocking | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| A **heroic** item trait | **25 Heroic Unlockstones** |
| A **regular** item trait | **100 regular Unlockstones** |

Two consequences that change advice:

- **A weekly-capped currency cannot be ground.** There is no "farm harder" answer — only "do not
  miss weeks", and a plan that spends the budget in the right order. Say this plainly, exactly as
  with the Flame of Purification cap.
- **Never recommend unlocking a trait without saying what it costs in Unlockstones**, and never
  recommend spending them on a piece the player is about to replace. A trait unlocked on gear that
  gets swapped is a week of a capped currency gone.

Heroic traits are also *selected* on unlock rather than rolled, so the choice is permanent for that
stone spend — which makes matching the target build's pick before spending especially important.

# Runes and artifacts — the two systems players most often leave empty

These are the biggest routinely-unclaimed sources of power, and both are invisible on the character
sheet. **Ask to see them.** Players frequently have empty artifact slots, artifacts from mismatched
sets, or runes that are slotted but in the wrong order.

If the imported target build specifies runes or artifacts, the player's should follow it. **If the
build specifies none, say so plainly** and give the general guidance below rather than pretending
there is a target to compare against.

## Artifacts — six slots, 14 sets, bonuses at 2 / 4 / 6 pieces

Six slots: **four Talistones, one Solarstone (active), one Lunarstone (passive)**.

There are **14 artifact sets**, every one of them six pieces with bonuses at **2, 4 and 6**. Example
(`Deathless One`): 2pc Block Chance Penetration +10%, 4pc `damage_reduction_penetration 24`, 6pc
Block Chance Penetration +15%.

- **A set is identifiable from the name prefix** — all six "Plains Ravager" pieces share one.
- **Mixing sets costs you the higher tiers.** Three pieces of one set and three of another gives two
  2-piece bonuses instead of a 6-piece, which is usually much worse.
- **No artifact set bonus grants raw attribute points** — checked across all 14. Their value is in
  derived stats and passives, so they do not compete with gear for the raw-stat-points priority.
- **Riftstones are named after field bosses** ("Adentus Riftstone"), so tonight's boss tells you
  which riftstone is farmable tonight.

Empty artifact slots are among the cheapest real gains available, because the pieces drop from
ordinary content rather than needing a specific best-in-slot roll.

## Runes — every socket should be filled, and the ORDER matters

Runes come in four types: **attack, defense, assist, and chaos** (a wildcard that counts as any type
for synergy purposes). They level by consuming duplicates, so rune progress is a volume problem
rather than a currency one — which is why rune headroom stays large even on well-geared characters.

**A rune's stat is a weighted random roll, not a choice.** Each rune has a pool of possible stats
with explicit probabilities. So never say "slot an attack rune for critical hit" — say "reroll until
you hit X", and be honest that it is a roll.

### Synergies — 78 of them, all grade 41, and order is the whole mechanic

A synergy is an **ordered arrangement** of attack/defense/assist across three sockets. Six per
equipment category — exactly 3! — across 13 categories.

**Two things follow, and both are easy to get wrong:**

1. **Synergies exist only at grade 41.** Telling a player with common runes to "arrange them for the
   synergy" is wrong; there is no synergy to get.
2. **The same three runes in a different sequence give a different bonus.** A recommendation must
   state the **sequence**, not the set.

The six weapon synergies, to show how different the orderings are:

| Order | Bonus |
| --- | --- |
| ATTACK ASSIST DEFENSE | **Dexterity +4**, Attack Speed +350 |
| ATTACK DEFENSE ASSIST | **Strength +4**, Critical Damage Taken 700 |
| DEFENSE ATTACK ASSIST | **Perception +4**, Shield Block Chance Penetration 1050 |
| DEFENSE ASSIST ATTACK | **Wisdom +4**, Debuff Taken Duration −350 |
| ASSIST DEFENSE ATTACK | Damage Reduction 24, Boss Damage Reduction 48 |
| ASSIST ATTACK DEFENSE | Boss Bonus Attack Power 32, Damage Reduction Penetration 24 |

**Four of the six grant +4 to a base attribute.** That is the headline: rune ordering is one of the
few levers that adds *raw attribute points*, which outrank derived stats in priority. A player with
grade-41 runes in a suboptimal order is leaving a free +4 attribute on the table — and reordering
costs nothing but the rearrangement.

Every category has its own six; the weapon table above is illustrative, not universal. Note the data
also contains a `test` category — a questlog artifact, ignore it.

## What to tell the player

- If artifact slots are **empty**: that is usually the single cheapest power available.
- If artifacts are from **mixed sets**: name the set they are closest to completing.
- If runes are **missing**: every empty socket is pure loss; a Rune Hammer unlocks one.
- If runes are present but the build specifies an order and theirs differs: state the **sequence**
  to move to, and that it is free.
- If the target build specifies neither: say so, explain what synergies are and that they need
  grade 41, and let the player decide whether to pursue them.
