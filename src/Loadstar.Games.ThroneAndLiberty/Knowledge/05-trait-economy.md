# The trait economy — Unlockstones, Sollant, and why this is the real bottleneck

Traits are where a geared character's power actually comes from, and they are gated by **two
separate scarce resources at once**: a weekly-capped stone, and Sollant in the **millions**. Advice
that names a trait upgrade without pricing both is not advice, it is a wish.

## Sollant costs — verified from the item database

| Action | Sollant | Materials |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Open** one Trait Resonance slot | **1,500,000** | 3 × Trait Resonance Stone |
| **Change** one Trait Resonance slot | **1,500,000** | 3 × Trait Resonance Stone |

Resonance has **four slots per item**. Opening all four on a **single piece** is therefore
**6,000,000 Sollant and 12 Trait Resonance Stones** — and changing a slot you mis-picked costs the
same again as opening it. There is no discount for a correction.

That is the number to put in front of a player before they commit: a resonance mistake is a
1.5-million-Sollant mistake.

## Unlockstone costs

| Unlocking | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| A **heroic** item trait | **25 Heroic Unlockstones** |
| A **regular** item trait | **100 regular Unlockstones** |

**Unlockstones are primarily weekly-limited**, which makes them the pacing constraint rather than a
price. A weekly cap cannot be ground away — the only levers are "do not miss weeks" and "spend in
the right order". Say that plainly, the same way the Flame of Purification cap is handled.

Heroic traits are **selected** on unlock rather than rolled, so the pick is committed the moment the
stones are spent. Match the target build's pick *before* spending.

## Where Unlockstones come from

Three routes, and only one is fully in the player's control.

**1. Weekly allotment.** The baseline, and it is capped.

**2. Lithographs — the partially grindable route.** A lithograph consumes several equipment pieces
and returns stone fragments. Verified example, "Garments of Quiet Wisdom": consumes 3 chest pieces
and outputs **6 × Trait Unlockstone Fragment** and **2 × Trait Resonance Stone Piece**. Fragments
then craft into a Trait Unlockstone together with Precious Magic Powder.

This matters for a specific, common recommendation: **surplus gear is not vendor trash.** A player
sitting on duplicate or superseded pieces is sitting on unlockstone fragments. That is often the
cheapest route to a trait unlock available to them, and it costs nothing they were using.

**3. Fortitude Coins from daily dungeon chests**, which craft into Unlockstones — but dungeon runs
are gated by **Dimensional Contract Token I and II**, of which there is a finite number per week. So
this route is *also* weekly-bounded; it raises the ceiling, it does not remove it.

There is additionally a **limited-time field boss PvP event** running as another partial source.
Being time-limited, it is worth prioritising while it lasts — but never build a long plan on it.

*(The 25/100 stone costs, the Fortitude Coin route, the Dimensional Contract Token gating and the
event are player-reported. The Sollant figures and the lithograph input/output above are read
directly from the item database.)*

## Enhancement stones — unlocking a trait is only step one

Unlocking a trait gets it at **one pip**. Raising it costs a different currency: **Trait Enhancement
Stones**, which carry **their own item level** and can only enhance equipment of the **same or lower**
level. Levelling consumes the experience points held in the stone.

That level restriction is a real, checkable blocker and it catches people out: **a stack of low-level
enhancement stones is useless on a high-level piece.** So "you have 40 enhancement stones" is not
enough information — their level matters as much as their count. Ask.

Regular Unlockstones also **convert upward into Heroic Trait Unlockstones** via a recipe, which is
why the two costs relate: 100 regular for a regular trait, 25 heroic for a heroic one.

## Answering "I only have N stones — what should I unlock first?"

This is the question the whole trait section exists to serve, and it has a definite method. Work it
in this order:

1. **Establish the budget.** How many regular Unlockstones, how many Heroic, how many Enhancement
   Stones **and at what item level**, and how much Sollant. **Ask if you do not know** — do not
   assume, and do not answer in the abstract. This is arithmetic, and arithmetic needs the numbers.
2. **Divide the budget by the unit cost** to get how many unlocks are actually affordable: 100
   regular stones is *one* regular trait. Say that plainly, because a player holding 100 stones may
   believe they can do several.
3. **Filter to slots the player is keeping.** A trait unlocked on a piece about to be replaced is
   the budget destroyed. If a slot is behind and due an upgrade, it is disqualified regardless of how
   good the trait would be.
4. **Rank the survivors by what the TARGET BUILD asks for.** The build states which traits it wants
   per slot; a trait the build does not name is not a candidate, however attractive it looks.
5. **Break ties by contribution size**, using the build's own trait values — the pip values are
   stated per item, so this is a lookup rather than a judgement.
6. **State the answer as one action with its full price**, and say what it leaves: "unlock Melee
   Endurance on your chest — 100 regular Unlockstones, your whole current stock, leaving nothing for
   the helm until next week."

If the budget cannot afford anything at all, **say so and name what to save toward** instead of
recommending a spend the player cannot make. That is still the most useful answer available.

## What this changes about advice

- **Always price a trait recommendation in BOTH currencies.** "Unlock this trait" is incomplete;
  "25 Heroic Unlockstones, about a week's supply" is advice.
- **Resonance is a millions-of-Sollant commitment, and correcting it costs the same as doing it.**
  Never suggest it casually, and never suggest it on a piece the player may replace.
- **Never recommend spending stones on gear that is about to be swapped.** That is a week of a
  capped currency destroyed, and it is an easy mistake to lead someone into by recommending a trait
  unlock and a gear upgrade in the same breath.
- **Check for surplus gear before recommending a grind.** Lithographs turn gear the player already
  has into the exact currency they are short of.
- Because every route is weekly-bounded, **the ordering of spend matters more than the total**. The
  right question is not "how do I get more stones" but "which unlock, first, gives the most".
