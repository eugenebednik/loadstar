# Where materials come from, and what a solo player cannot do

Two questions this answers: **what do I run to get the thing you just told me to spend**, and **is this
advice even available to me**. The second matters more than it looks — several of the richest systems in
this game are guild content that a solo player cannot enter at all, and recommending them is worse than
saying nothing.

## Growthstones — the material that raises item level

Four rarities, ascending: **Quality → Rare → Precious → Epic**. Separate stones per category —
**weapon, armour, accessory** — so a surplus of one does nothing for another. A player short on
accessory stones is short on accessory stones specifically.

Two sources:

- **Codex quests.** Available at every rarity. See below; this is the underrated one.
- **NPC crafters** — Weapons Crafter, Armor Crafter, Accessories Crafter, in Solisium's major cities.
- **Epic requires a SENIOR crafter**, which is not in every city. One is in Stonegard Castle.

So "you need three more Precious Armour Growthstones" has a real answer: codex quests, or the Armor
Crafter in a major city. If it is Epic, they need to travel to a senior crafter.

## The Codex is a materials source, not just a story

**Exploration Codex** rewards EXP and Sollant, and also **Morphstones and Growthstones** — the exact
material that raises item level. **Adventure Codex** is the main storyline and unlocks regions and
characters. Both also feed Stellar Journey.

**This makes codex completion a gear action, and it almost never gets recommended as one.** A player
stuck for Growthstones who has unfinished exploration codex entries has stones waiting that cost
nothing but travel. Worth checking before suggesting they farm.

## Dimensional Trials — TIERS, not stars, and they are the rune source

**Do not call these star ratings.** Stars belong to the older co-op dungeons, and a patch note about a
"final 2-Star dungeon" is about those, not these. Dimensional Trials use **Tiers**, where Tier 1 sits at
roughly standard co-op difficulty but pays better.

A co-op dungeon scored on accumulated points, rewarding by total. Clearing yields a **Rune Chance
Chest** whose rarity follows the tier, Rare being the best, plus new runes, achievements and seasonal
ranking. Entry is gated by **Dimensional Contract Tokens**, finite per week.

**This is the most actionable link in this file.** Runes were the single largest Combat Power gap on the
reference character — 988 of 1530, more headroom than any other system — and rune progression is a
volume problem, because levelling one consumes duplicates. Dimensional Trials are where that volume
comes from. If the Combat Power tooltip shows runes as the biggest gap, the answer is a tier of
Dimensional Trials, not a vague "get more runes".

## Guild level — and why "do Guild Raids" is often useless advice

Two Guild Raids are gated at **Guild Level 30**. Guild level rises from:

- **Guild Contracts**, which unlock by guild level: Territory Mobs at 1, Dynamic Event at 2, Open World
  Dungeons at 3.
- **Member donations** and Activity Points, from the whole guild, daily.

**The exact experience curve to 30 is not published** — treat any specific number as unverified.

**What matters for advice: a player cannot fix this themselves.** Guild level is the accumulated daily
activity of everyone in the guild. Telling someone in a small or quiet guild to run Guild Raids is
telling them to change other people's habits, which is not a next action. Ask whether their guild is
already at 30 before building any recommendation on it, and if it is not, recommend something else.

## Boonstones — guild-only, two guilds per war, and a trap for stat reading

Boonstones are **Holdstones captured by guilds, one per Territory**, contested in a **Boonstone War** —
king-of-the-hill guild PvP.

How a war happens:

1. A guild declares intent **12 to 36 hours** before the scheduled battle.
2. **12 hours out, the system picks exactly two guilds**: the current owner as defender, and the
   highest-ranked guild that signed up.
3. The Guild Leader, Advisor or Guardian must hold a **Guild Flag**, bought at the Guild Supplies Shop.

The controlling guild gets **stat bonuses and hourly resources**. The bonus differs per boonstone —
Skill Damage Boost, Hit Boost, Critical Hit Boost, Mana Regeneration, Defense.

### Two consequences, and the second is a real reading hazard

**It is unavailable to almost everyone.** Only two guilds take part in a given war, and only guilds at
all. For a solo or guildless player this is not content; it is scenery. Never list it as a next action
without knowing they are in a guild that contests boonstones.

**AND IT INFLATES THE CHARACTER SHEET.** A boonstone bonus is active only while the guild holds the
stone, so a player whose guild currently controls one is reading stats that include a buff they will
lose. Advice priced against those numbers is wrong the moment control changes — and the character sheet
does not label which part is a boonstone. If a stat looks unexpectedly high for the gear on display,
that is a reason to ask, not to build a plan on it.

## Still not captured

- **Guild-level experience values** — the curve to 30, so "how long until we unlock raids" stays
  unanswerable.
- **Growthstone quantities per craft**, and the material cost of crafting one.
- **Dimensional Trial tier list** — how many tiers exist and the chest rarity at each.
- **Which boonstone grants which bonus**, by name. Six exist and are named in the roster; the mapping
  from stone to stat is not recorded.
